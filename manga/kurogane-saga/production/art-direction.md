# Manga Art Direction and Continuity Guide

## Overall visual language

- Original Japanese-inspired fantasy manga.
- Black-and-white interior pages with screentones, clear value grouping, expressive faces, and strong silhouette design.
- Right-to-left page and panel reading order.
- Pre-modern architecture, clothes, weapons, props, and daily life.
- Action should be cinematic but legible. Keep the sword, body position, target, and direction of movement readable in each exchange.
- Use quiet panels after loud panels. A reveal needs space; not every panel should be filled with effects.
- Establish page turns around reveals, reversals, and punchlines.
- Text and speech bubbles should be typeset separately from generated art whenever possible. Do not rely on image models to spell dialogue accurately.

## Character continuity

### Ren Kurogane
- Age 16 at opening.
- Messy, layered black hair; dark charcoal eyes.
- Lean, practical, trained through village work and repeated sword practice, not already a finished warrior.
- Early outfit: charcoal training kimono, plain dark trousers, cloth hand wraps, worn sandals.
- Weapon at opening: wooden training sword. A proper katana comes later when his means and training justify it.
- Face design: youthful, readable eyebrows, often blank or confused in comedy, focused when someone is in danger.
- Black is the dominant identity colour in colour art; monochrome art must distinguish the outfit through value and texture.
- Do not draw him as a cold, flawless genius. His combat adaptability and everyday stupidity need to coexist.

### Tori Tsukishiro
- Age 16 at opening; A-Rank.
- Long crimson-red hair, ruby eyes, elegant but athletic posture.
- Early combat clothing: practical crimson-and-black academy uniform inspired by traditional Japanese garments; armour and accessories should be functional, not a fantasy ball gown.
- Royal hair ornament and a refined katana with a crimson-lacquered sheath.
- Expression range is crucial: composed public face, competitive stare, immediate outrage, flustered denial, and the softness she tries to hide.
- Her current design should read as a capable teen swordfighter and princess, not be sexualised. After an adult timeskip, the design can mature in a restrained and consistent way.

### Daichi Moriyama
- Broad silhouette, short dark-brown hair, warm eyes; earth-toned durable clothes and a heavy sword.
- His silhouette should be visibly heavier and more grounded than Ren's.

### Yuna Shirogane
- Shoulder-length silver hair, sharp grey eyes, compact agile frame; travel-ready layers and twin short swords.
- Her design uses clean angles and small metal details rather than large decorative pieces.

### Haru Minazuki
- Straight dark-navy hair, cool blue-grey eyes, tall narrow silhouette; refined deep-blue clothing and a long katana.
- His poses are precise and economical.

## Panel and page craft

- Each panel must advance action, character, atmosphere, or information. Remove panels that do none of those things.
- Avoid overloading a page with exposition. Let gestures, background details, costume symbols, and reactions communicate information.
- Use varied shot sizes: establishing wides, medium blocking, close-up expressions, hands and weapons, and occasional full-page images.
- Keep dialogue short enough to fit balloons comfortably. Do not put several paragraphs in one speech bubble.
- SFX are design elements, but use them with restraint and keep dialogue readable.
- Keep character positions consistent from panel to panel. If a character changes side or direction, make the movement clear.
- Action choreography must specify who is where, what is moving, and why the next beat follows.
- A full-page reveal should earn its space.

## Production pipeline

1. Lock written character reference.
2. Generate or draw approved character turnarounds and expressions.
3. Storyboard the chapter in right-to-left reading order.
4. Build rough panels and verify spatial continuity.
5. Finish line art, inks, blacks, and screentones.
6. Add lettering as a separate, editable layer.
7. Review character model consistency, dialogue, panel flow, and page-turn logic.
8. Export a readable digital chapter and archive sources.

The first image generation is a design reference, not a finished manga page. Keep that distinction clear.
