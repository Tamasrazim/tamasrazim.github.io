The Last Star Train

On her first night as a signal keeper, young fox Eira joins the Starfall Express, a little sky train that carries star-lanterns between floating towns. When a storm knocks out the route lights, Eira, her lantern-moth Pip and the dragon pilot Nix must find the way home across a marsh boardwalk, a shifting forest, an ice viaduct, a crowded sky market, a windmill canyon and a tunnel with a vanishing-point track. Each place teaches them a different way to read the route. The final rail ends at a gulf, so the crew must build a path of light together.

THE STORY, FROM FIRST LIGHT TO LAST

Eira thinks a good signal keeper should already know every route. The blackout proves otherwise. At Mosswater she learns not to trust a reflection; in Whisperwood the safest route appears only from above; on the ice viaduct the crew must call turns together. The sky market tests their timing, the engine room combines all three clues, and the observatory reveals the hidden tunnel. When the rails finally end over Moonfall Gap, none of them can cross alone. Their linked lanterns become a temporary track, bringing the Express safely to First-Light Harbor at sunrise.

HOW THIS CARTOON IS BUILT

The film combines hand-drawn 2D characters with layered faux-3D perspective: a side-on railway platform, a boardwalk above real water, a winding forest corridor, a deep ice viaduct, a multi-level market, a gear-filled interior, an open canyon, a circular overhead star chart and a vanishing-point tunnel. The train tracks, floor planes and walkable decks are painted before the characters, so the performers sit on the scene instead of hovering over it. Its 115 authored camera setups include wide shots, close reactions, moving chase views and overhead map imagery. Every frame is deterministic for repeatable scrubbing.

The film includes timed on-screen dialogue and narration cues, but no recorded voice or music track. It is drawn by JavaScript Canvas rather than a pre-rendered video file; use EXPORT 4K VIDEO to capture playback in a browser-supported MP4 or WebM format.
